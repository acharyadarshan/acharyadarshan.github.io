Research Interests

My research interest lies in building wireless systems that remain robust when real-world conditions violate clean modeling assumptions. Specifically, I am interested in how handsets can use low-fidelity, cheap-to-observe signals—coarse CQI reports, HARQ retransmission patterns, thermal readings, modem state transitions—to adapt physical-layer and scheduling parameters under non-stationary channels, without relying on expensive measurements or ideal feedback loops.

Topics I want to explore include:

  • Adaptive link and PHY configuration using indirect proxy signals for regime-shift detection under mobility and interference.
  • Cross-layer failure modeling—understanding how PHY and upper-layer interactions produce long-tail stalls and throughput collapse that average-case metrics hide.
  • Evaluation methodology that surfaces tail behavior and reproducible edge-case failures, rather than only reporting means.

My applied background in RF drive testing, device validation, and embedded systems gives me a measurement-first perspective: I have seen the gap between what models assume and what devices actually do in the field, and I want to work on methods that close it in a principled, reproducible way.
